Three buckets. All three are fixable.

We're not looking for a better rate. We're looking for money that doesn't need to leave in the first place.

01

Card fees you're absorbing

California allows a compliant cash-price / card-price program with proper signage and receipt disclosure. Cash and debit customers pay the shelf price; card customers cover the cost of the card. Structured correctly, the $9,989 a month stops coming out of your margin. Straight Answers

The questions you're going to ask.

Will customers get mad about a card price?

Some will notice, almost nobody leaves. The programs that fail are the ones with a surprise line item at the bottom of the check. The ones that work post the cash price and the card price on the menu and the door, train the staff on a one-sentence answer, and never argue about it — if a regular objects, you comp the difference and move on. We handle the signage and the training script.

Is this even legal in California?

Yes, with conditions that matter. California requires clear up-front disclosure of the price difference, and debit card transactions can't be surcharged under federal rules regardless of what any salesperson tells you. Any program that ignores those two things is a liability, not a savings plan. We implement it the compliant way and put the disclosure language in writing — and we'd tell you to ask the other guy how he handles debit.

What happens to my Toast data and gift card balances?

Outstanding gift card liability transfers — we reconcile the balance file before cutover so no customer walks in with a card that doesn't work. Sales history, menu and customer data get exported and imported. This is the part most switches botch, which is why it happens on a Tuesday with both systems available.

How is this different from what Alex Hult is pitching?

On the payments mechanism, honestly, it may not be — dual pricing is dual pricing. The difference is everything around it: you're getting a real website, ordering, SEO and someone who knows your name. And Kyle vouched for us to you, which means he's on the hook for how this goes. That's not nothing.

What if I hate it in three months?

No long-term processing contract and no early-termination fee. The website is yours on your own domain regardless. We'd rather you stay because the numbers are good than because you're stuck.

What does the website cost?

Nothing, if the payments relationship comes with it. We're paid on processing, so the site, the ordering setup, the SEO and the ongoing changes come out of that side. If you only want the website, we'll quote it — but the whole point is that you shouldn't have to pay for it twice.
